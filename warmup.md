# Chinese warm-up

These readings distinguish what the author says from what is known to be true.

| Message | Literal English | Intended meaning and feeling |
|---|---|---|
| 策划是懂玩家的，懂得怎么悄悄改概率 | “The designers understand players: they know how to quietly change the probabilities.” | Sarcastic praise becomes an accusation of secretly changing gacha odds. The player feels suspicious, angry or betrayed. It is an allegation, not proof of changed probabilities. |
| 这波属于是逼氪了，懂的都懂 | “This move amounts to forcing spending; those who know, know.” | The player feels that progress or competitiveness now pressures them to pay. 氪 means spending money in-game; 逼氪 is perceived coercive monetisation. The tone is frustrated, cynical and invites shared understanding. |
| 艾琳=铁锅，新人别懵 | “Ailin equals iron pot; newcomers, don't be confused.” | Ailin is being identified by the community nickname ‘iron pot.’ The author is explaining slang, likely casually or playfully. This sentence alone does not explain the nickname's origin or whether it expresses praise or criticism. |
| 白嫖党表示毫无压力 [doge] | “The free-to-play crowd reports no pressure whatsoever [doge].” | 白嫖党 here means players who do not spend. The doge marker makes the claim playful or ironic: it could be smug indifference or masking frustration. Parent context is needed before deciding whether it is genuine confidence or sarcastic criticism. |
| 非酋报道，160抽一个金都没有 绷不住了 | “An unlucky player reporting in: 160 pulls and not one gold; I can't hold it together.” | 非酋 is self-deprecating slang for bad gacha luck; 金 refers to a gold/high-rarity outcome. The player is disappointed and exasperated, perhaps using exaggeration or humour. No pity-system rules are supplied, so this does not independently prove a bug or manipulated odds. |
